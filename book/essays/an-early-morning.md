# An early morning's work

*2026-11-15. A short look back at this morning: a cookbook for the book, a spike, a landing page shipped, and the warts it showed in Shale.*

## The book found a touchstone

The morning began where last night ended, with the book. Your thought about cookbooks led to the menu riff, and to the line you drew under it: the reader is inside the restaurant, answerable for every plate, so the metaphor lends lessons and doesn't set the structure. Then The Food Lab became the touchstone beside Moby Dick and K&R, because cooking as experiment fits a practice that's still evolving. The five-chapter spike followed, organized by kinds of work: searching, changing many files, gating, cleaning up a box, and reading your own agent. Each chapter is a principle, an experiment from our record, and a recipe, written somewhere between a column and a book.

## Then a real project

The landing page was the first new project since the spike, and it turned out to be the spike's kind of work: real web work, where Shale never built the product but made every claim on the page checkable. It went from a dusty grid to "Steve's Roc Work" in about an hour of back and forth:

- **Five featured programs, one to a row,** each with its stats beside it, a "Roc at work" line naming one strength the code really shows, and a "Roc plays nice with others" line.

- **Luke's six, credited properly,** as toy retro games he wrote and this site ported to the web, with his copyright notice now kept in the repo.

- **Shipped to roc.lynrummy.com,** every one of its 697 files proved identical to staging.

Two habits did most of the work. Four cold agents proposed a pitch each from the code, and three more reviewed the page for Roc, for your work, and for clear disclosure that agents wrote the code. And every claim was checked before it went on the page: two came off because a check said no, and the license line said UPL, not GPL, because the LICENSE files did.

## What your corrections taught

Most of your replies were small and sharp, and each one is a rule worth keeping:

- **A sentence, not a fragment,** at the top of anything. That one is in memory now.

- **Accurate beats precise.** The intro first undersold itself, then said too much, before it said the right amount.

- **Lineage people know.** Safari's roots are told through TypeScript, not Codex, which nobody has heard of.

- **Check before you assume, both ways.** You were right that the browser sees JavaScript and wasm, and the code was right that a small Zig host sits inside the wasm; the page now says both.

## And Shale got better for it

The page's rough spots became Shale's fixes the same morning, so I'll only list them. The 64-program cap became a budget, and the recipes built around it were freed. Shale gained `copy` and regex counts, a `..` stop that names the fix, a HEAD request that no longer hangs, and two recipes. The consumers gate caught one parse site I'd missed, and passed on the second run. The essay on the landing page has the detail.

## The rhythm

It was a good example of the cadence we talked about last night: a little book thinking, then building something real, then fixing the tools where the building rubbed, and then back.
