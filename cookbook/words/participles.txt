-- Past participles a name hunt cannot find by its rule (more than three
-- letters ending in `ed`, ed_verbs.txt excepted): English's irregular
-- ones, a closed set, written out by hand. A first word here names a state,
-- not an act: `blown : Str -> Bool` asks no question, `held_raw!` does no
-- deed. One word a line; `--` starts a note.
--
-- Left out, so a name starting with one is never taken for a state:
--   a participle that is its own base form, which names the act: bet, bid,
--   broadcast, burst, cast, come, become, overcome, cost, cut, fit,
--   forecast, hit, hurt, input, knit, let, offset, output, preset, put,
--   quit, read, reread, reset, rid, run, outrun, overrun, set, upset,
--   shed, shut, slit, split, spread, thrust, typeset, wed, wet (`read!`
--   reads; `reset!` resets);
--   a form that is also another word's base or a common noun or
--   adjective: bound (to bound), felt (the cloth), found (to found),
--   ground (to ground, the ground), left (the side), rent (to rent),
--   rung (a ladder's), shot (a shot), smelt (to smelt), spelt (the
--   grain), thought (a thought), wound (to wound, a wound);
--   a form that is only a past tense, never a participle (forgot, hid,
--   lay, wrote).
